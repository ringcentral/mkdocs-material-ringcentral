# Voice & Tone

**Voice** is who we are — our consistent personality across all product copy. **Tone** is how we adapt that personality to context. Our voice stays constant; our tone shifts to match the moment.

---

## Our voice

RingCentral's voice is **clear, confident, and human**. We speak directly to the person in front of us — not to a generic "user" — with the warmth of a knowledgeable colleague who respects their time.

### Clear

We use plain language. We don't use jargon when a simple word works. We write short sentences. We prefer active voice.

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| "Utilize the functionality provided by the connector" | "Use the connector" |
| "An error has been encountered" | "Something went wrong" |
| "Navigate to the Settings interface" | "Go to Settings" |

### Confident

We know what we're talking about. We don't hedge unnecessarily or overqualify. We make recommendations.

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| "You might want to consider trying…" | "Try this:" |
| "This might possibly be due to…" | "This is usually caused by…" |
| "We're sorry for any inconvenience" | "We're on it." |

### Human

We're talking to people, not computers. We use contractions. We acknowledge frustration. We celebrate when things go right.

| Instead of | Write |
|------------|-------|
| "The operation completed successfully." | "All done!" or "Saved." |
| "An error has been detected in your input." | "That email address doesn't look right." |
| "User credentials are invalid." | "Incorrect password. Try again, or reset it." |

---

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| **Onboarding** | Warm, encouraging, motivating | "You're almost set up. Just one more step." |
| **Success states** | Celebratory but brief | "Connected! Your calls will start logging shortly." |
| **Error states** | Calm, helpful, solution-oriented | "We couldn't save your changes. Check your connection and try again." |
| **Destructive actions** | Direct, serious, no softening | "This will permanently delete all connector data." |
| **Loading** | Neutral, informative | "Loading your connectors…" |
| **Empty states** | Friendly, forward-looking | "No connectors yet. Add one to start logging calls." |
| **Tooltips / helper text** | Precise, minimal | "Include country code for international numbers." |
| **Security / compliance** | Clear, no-nonsense | "We never store your CRM password. OAuth tokens are encrypted at rest." |

## What we are not

Understanding what our voice is *not* is as important as knowing what it is.

| We are not… | We are… |
|-------------|---------|
| Snarky or ironic | Direct and warm |
| Overly formal or corporate | Accessible and human |
| Cutesy or playful in serious moments | Appropriately serious when needed |
| Apologetic (repeated sorrys) | Action-oriented |
| Jargon-heavy | Plain-spoken |
| Passive | Active and confident |

---

## When tone shifts

The biggest tone shifts happen around **errors and destructive actions**.

For everyday interactions, lean warm and casual. As consequences increase, shift toward directness and clarity — not coldness, but precision. The user needs accurate information, not reassurance.

```
Casual:   "All done! Your connector is set up."
↓
Neutral:  "Sync paused — re-authenticate to continue."
↓
Serious:  "This will permanently delete your connector and all associated data. This cannot be undone."
```

---

## Voice checklist

Before finalizing any copy:

- [ ] Is it in plain language? Could a 14-year-old understand it?
- [ ] Is it active voice? (Subject does the action, not the other way around.)
- [ ] Is it specific? Does it say exactly what happened / what to do?
- [ ] Does the tone match the context? (Serious enough for errors, warm enough for success.)
- [ ] Does it sound like a person, not a system?
